Plate 02The problemBetween offsites

02

Agreed in a workshop. Filed in a drawer.

The market keeps moving after the offsite. Nobody checks the plan against it until the annual review, and by then a correction is expensive. News arrives with no link to what the team said must succeed. Meetings follow the loudest voice, and decisions disappear into slides.

Teams fail in two ways. With inertia they ignore every signal. With thrash they react to all of them.

Plate 03The structureRead against one Ambition

Twelve criteria. Four in focus.

03

Four criteria get the team's full attention. The rest stay tracked.

Each portfolio has one Ambition, with targets such as turnover, margin and a year if you want them. Under it sit 7 to 12 success criteria. Each criterion shows today's light in green, yellow or red. Up to four become focus areas.

Each criterion holds 2 to 4 objectives. Each activity sits under one objective and names an action, a result, a timeframe and the person responsible.

Start from a workshop report, a document, a pasted email or a plain prompt. Vivo proposes where each item belongs, and an Owner accepts it.

Plate 04The radarWatch · triage · move

04

Vivo proposes the move. A person confirms it.

For each focus area the Radar reads the outside world. Its search brief is the criterion, its objectives and its risks. Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports on Monday and Thursday.

Vivo assigns each signal to a criterion with one line of reason. A delegated person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Vivo learns from dismissed signals what your team counts as noise.

When a signal shifts a risk, Vivo draws the new position as a ghost. A move into the top right needs at least one primary source, so that one blog post can't raise an alarm. A risk that stays there for 14 days without a decision goes to the session lead's Needs you list.

Plate 05The sessionWeekly, fortnightly or monthly

Sixty minutes. Everyone takes a position first.

05

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session from what changed: new signals, risks entering the top right, parked questions that are due, activities off track and asks from the people responsible. Old business comes first. Every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits it, and the room approves it as step one.

For each item the team sees the evidence and writes a sealed first take. The takes are revealed together, so nobody hears the loudest voice before giving their own. Then the team discusses the item. Vivo can estimate the cost of doing nothing, sketch a scenario, argue the other side or name a black swan.

Plate 06The five optionsOne per agenda item

06

Every item ends in one of five decisions.

The bar shows how much each option changes. Holding is a decision too, and it gets a date.

  1. a

    Hold

    The criterion is marked reviewed on that date.

  2. b

    Adapt

    Vivo drafts changes to the objectives and activities.

  3. c

    Pivot

    Vivo drafts new wording for the criterion. It takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.

  4. d

    Take advantage

    The team adds detour activities. The criterion stays as it is.

  5. e

    Not yet

    The team names what is missing. The question is parked with a trigger that brings it back, such as new evidence, a date or the next session. Or the team lets it go and records why.

Plate 07The recordPermanent numbers

07

Every decision gets a number.

Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions, including dissent by name with its reason. The session lead confirms it, and the entry keeps its number for good.

Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver in the app, as an exported memo or into the board pack. Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner. Then Strapivo prompts the lead to announce what changed and why. The plan and the board pack export as Markdown, and the plan as CSV.
