# 61. Park it properly

- Range: Third round: landing page candidates
- Audience: Leadership teams stuck between premature calls and endless procrastination
- Colours: House palette, plus warm sand (#F3EDE0) for parked state cards and muted ochre (#B5831D) for return triggers.

## The task

Build a landing page for Strapivo as a single `index.html` in this folder. It is one of twenty third-round concepts. Each is meant to work as Strapivo's real main landing page, and each must look and read differently from the other 19 and from the 50 pages of rounds one and two.

## The concept

Every executive team knows the worst outcome of a strategy meeting: "Let's revisit this later." Later never comes, or the exact same argument restarts three months later. In Strapivo, "Not yet" is a formal decision option with teeth. The room asks "What is missing?" and either **Parks** the question with at least one explicit trigger that brings it back (new evidence, a named person's view, a specific date, or the next session) or **Lets it go** with a written rationale, "so that it feels like parking, not losing."

**Hero.** Balanced two-column desktop hero. Left column: headline "Decide now, or name what brings the question back." Two plain sentences explaining Strapivo: the Strategic Design System that keeps your leadership team's agreed strategy current, turns live market signals into a 60-minute weekly agenda and ensures every strategic question ends with a clear decision or a timed return trigger. Primary call to action. Right column: Drawing 1, a large crisp **Parked Question card** (labelled "Illustrative example") showing a real mid-market decision ("Enter Nordic direct distribution?"), Outcome: `Not yet · Parked`, Missing: "Margin impact from Q2 freight contract", Return trigger: `Evidence lands OR 15 June 2026`, Status: `Automatically returned to Thursday agenda · SDS-2026-014`.

**The page.** At most three product drawings total. Sections in order:
1. Why "let's revisit later" breaks strategy. Unresolved items either vanish into old minutes or return uninvited every time a new slide deck lands ("We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands").
2. Two clean exits from "Not yet". Drawing 2: a clean two-path diagram splitting "Not yet" into **Parked** (names what is missing + attaches one of four triggers: evidence, a person's view, a date, or next session) and **Let go** (closed deliberately with written rationale, so the team never re-argues it by accident).
3. All five options in one 60-minute loop. Briefly show the full set (Hold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage, Not yet), preceded by the Radar watching focus areas, the 24-hour drafted agenda (old business first, including returning parked items), and sealed first takes.
4. Human authority and trust. Vivo proposes; people decide. EU-only infrastructure, GDPR by design.
5. Getting started (four to five hours, bring your existing documents) and closing call to action.

**Tone and look.** Warm, composed, intelligent. Paper and sand alternating cleanly, ochre reserved for trigger lines, crisp mono timestamps and generous whitespace. Do not use "over 4,000 sources a day" on this page.
