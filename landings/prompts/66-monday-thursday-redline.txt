# 66. Monday, Thursday, red line

- Range: Third round: landing page candidates
- Audience: Time-poor CEOs and owner-managers who reject constant notifications
- Colours: House palette, plus whisper-light grey (#ECEFF3) for quiet days and a sharp crimson (#C93B2B) reserved strictly for the single red-line exception.

## The task

Build a landing page for Strapivo as a single `index.html` in this folder. It is one of twenty third-round concepts. Each is meant to work as Strapivo's real main landing page, and each must look and read differently from the other 19 and from the 50 pages of rounds one and two.

## The concept

Time-poor executives refuse to install another tool that pings them twenty times a day. Strapivo protects executive attention by design: its market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day against your focus areas, stays silent on quiet days, and reports on a calm twice-weekly rhythm — Monday and Thursday. Only a red-line exception breaks that schedule.

(Note: this is the second of ONLY TWO pages in round three explicitly authorized to use "over 4,000 sources a day".)

**Hero.** Crisp Swiss-minimal editorial header with headline, two plain sentences explaining Strapivo and primary CTA, sitting directly over a bold, clean full-width **7-day rhythm strip** visible above the fold. Headline: "Quiet five days a week. Urgent only when it matters." Subtitle: Strapivo is the Strategic Design System that watches the market against your agreed strategy, briefs you on Monday and Thursday, and interrupts only for a red-line exception.
Drawing 1 (the hero 7-day strip, labelled "Illustrative example"): seven crisp columns (Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat, Sun). Mon and Thu carry clean Azure briefing pills ("Briefing · 2 signals touching Focus Area 1"); Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat, Sun are deliberately serene and quiet; across Wednesday sits one sharp hairline crimson exception indicator ("Red-line exception · Primary source confirmed · Interrupts schedule").

**The page.** At most three product drawings total. Sections in order:
1. Why constant alerts kill strategic thinking. News arrives as noise every hour. When everything looks urgent, executives mute the channel and fall back to the annual offsite.
2. How the quiet rhythm works. Drawing 2: a clean three-step cadence visual — Monday & Thursday briefings (filtered against up to 4 focus areas, dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise), Red-line exception (primary source required to enter the danger zone), and the 60-minute strategic session (agenda drafted 24 hours before).
3. One focused hour to decide. Sealed first takes before anyone speaks, five clear options (Hold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage, Not yet), and a permanent numbered Record of Decisions (SDS-2026-014).
4. Human authority and European trust. Vivo proposes; people decide. EU-only infrastructure, GDPR by design.
5. Onboarding ("We do the reading first", four to five hours, then you run it) and closing call to action.

**Tone and look.** Ultra-calm, Swiss-minimal, high restraint. Generous Paper space, crisp vertical rules, crimson used with surgical discipline so silence itself feels like a design choice.
