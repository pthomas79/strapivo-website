01of 10 · Home

Strategy needs a home.

Finance has a ledger. Sales has a pipeline. Strategy lives in a slide deck and the chief executive's head.

In Strapivo

The strategy sits in one place, built on your Business Model Canvas. Each portfolio has one Ambition and 7 to 12 success criteria. Each criterion has its objectives, and each objective has its activities. Risks sit on a radar of impact against likelihood.

A brand design system gives every team one logo and one palette. Strapivo gives every decision one strategy to read against.

  • Success criteria
  • Objectives
  • Activities
  • Risks
02of 10 · Cadence

A strategy nobody opens is a document.

Most strategies are agreed at an offsite and then filed. The market keeps moving. Nobody notices until the annual review, when a correction costs the most.

In Strapivo

The leadership team meets in the Strategy Room for 60 minutes, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. Each session starts from what moved since the last one.

Mission Control is the home screen. It shows the next session with a countdown, what needs you, every success criterion and the recent decisions.

  • Next session
  • Needs you
  • Success criteria
  • Recent decisions
03of 10 · Focus

Fill a few glasses to the brim.

A team that works on twelve priorities at once moves none of them far. Pick a few and put the effort there.

In Strapivo

You flag up to four success criteria as focus areas. Try to add a fifth and Strapivo asks: "You're diluting focus and creating noise. Do you really want to?"

The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area.

4 of 12 success criteria in focus
04of 10 · Watch

Watch what you said must succeed. Ignore the rest.

News arrives as noise. A competitor's announcement means nothing until you know which of your goals it touches.

In Strapivo

The Radar reads over 4,000 sources a day. Its search brief for each focus area is the success criterion, its objectives and its risks. Vivo, the AI in Strapivo, makes a first pass on every signal with one line of reason.

A person you delegate marks each signal relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

Signal states
  • New
  • Relevant
  • Watching
  • Dismissed
05of 10 · Agenda

Build the agenda from what changed.

A standing agenda repeats last month's meeting. The item that matters turns up under any other business, with four minutes left.

In Strapivo

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before each session from new signals, risks entering the top right of the risk radar, parked questions that are due back, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible.

Old business comes first. Every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits the draft and the room approves it as step one.

06of 10 · First takes

Write your position down before the loudest voice speaks.

The first opinion in a room becomes the anchor. Everyone who speaks after it argues with it or around it.

In Strapivo

Each person gives a sealed first take on the agenda item. Strapivo reveals all of them at once, and only then does the conversation start.

Vivo can raise the cost of doing nothing, a scenario, a challenge, an alternative or a black swan.

Every agenda item
  • Evidence
  • Sealed first takes
  • Conversation with Vivo
  • Decision
07of 10 · Decide

There are five answers.

A meeting that ends with "let's take that offline" has decided nothing. Give the team five answers and it will choose one.

In Strapivo

Hold marks the success criterion as reviewed on that date. Adapt has Vivo draft changes to objectives and activities. Take advantage adds detour activities and leaves the criterion as it is.

Pivot has Vivo draft new wording for the criterion. It takes effect when someone with authority confirms it.

The five answers
  • Hold
  • Adapt
  • Pivot
  • Take advantage
  • Not yet
08of 10 · Not yet

"Not yet" needs a trigger.

Deferring a question is fine. Most deferred questions never return, because nobody wrote down what would bring them back.

In Strapivo

Not yet starts with one question: what's missing? It ends as Parked, with at least one trigger that brings it back. A trigger is new evidence, a person's view, a date or the next session.

Or it ends as Let go, with a written rationale, so the team can see later why it dropped the question.

Two ways to close
  • Parked, with a trigger
  • Let go, with a rationale
09of 10 · Record

Dissent belongs on the record.

Minutes keep the outcome and lose the argument. A year later nobody can say who disagreed or why, and the team restarts the same debate.

In Strapivo

Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions, with dissent by name and its reason. The session lead confirms. Every decision gets a permanent number.

Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver in the app, into an exported memo or into the board pack.

10of 10 · Trust

Vivo proposes. People decide.

An AI that rewrites your strategy cannot be trusted with it. Leadership teams tell us they want "help, not a co-author with the pen."

In Strapivo

Vivo proposes a new position for a risk as a ghost on the radar, with a one-line reason. A person confirms or rejects it. A move into the top right, the danger zone, needs at least one primary source, so one blog post can't raise an alarm.

Vivo asks "Light still green?" and never changes the light itself. Every recommendation traces back to the strategic memory you approved. Strapivo runs on EU-only infrastructure and follows GDPR by design.
