## The concept

Built on one executive sentence: "I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait." The page is about the two ways companies get this wrong, inertia and thrash, and the narrow practice between them.

Structure: a hero that sets the quote large; a section on inertia (the strategy in the drawer, the annual review that arrives too late to correct cheaply); a section on thrash (reacting to every headline, reopening settled arguments); then the middle path: small, early corrections. Explain the mechanisms that keep a team out of each ditch. Against inertia: the radar, the 14-day no-decision nudge, "Light still green?", the trend word and time held that show pressure building before anything moves. Against thrash: the primary-source rule for the danger zone, triage, focus areas capped at four, Hold as a real decision, Parked and Let go, closed questions that stay closed. End on Vivo's prompt in the session: the cost of doing nothing.

Signature element: a wide SVG of a course line between two banks, inertia on one side and thrash on the other. A slider lets the visitor drag a company's behaviour from one bank to the other and the drawn path changes: a flat line that drifts off course, a jagged zigzag, or a steady line with small corrections. No numbers, no invented costs. The diagram is labelled as a sketch.
