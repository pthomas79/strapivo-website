Strapivo · Dynamic Strategy

Ask us the hard questions.

Every other function got software. Strategy never did. Strapivo is where a company's strategy lives, stays current and gets used. Dynamic Strategy is how a leadership team keeps that strategy honest between offsites, with small, early corrections and the rare change of course.

You have heard AI claims before, so this page has no feature tour. It has the eighteen questions executives put to us in the first meeting, with the short answer first.

  • We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands.

About the AI

01 / 18

Will Vivo rewrite our strategy?

No.

Vivo proposes. People decide.

Vivo is Strapivo's AI. When a decision calls for new wording on a success criterion, Vivo drafts it. The change takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.

Vivo never sets the green, yellow or red light on a criterion. When two or more focus activities go off track, or a risk on that criterion enters the danger zone, Vivo asks Light still green? A person answers.

Leadership teams tell us they want help, not a co-author with the pen. That is the design.

02 / 18

Isn't this a chatbot with our PDF attached?

No.

Strapivo is hardwired to your business model, built on the Business Model Canvas.

Vivo works from your strategic memory: the business model and its environment, the principles and guardrails for every call, where you play and where you don't. Your Owners approved every line of that memory, and every recommendation Vivo makes traces back to it.

Vivo can work as Auditor, Advisor, Scout, Colleague or Researcher. As Auditor it asks Where did this number come from? Any AI can answer a question. Only Strapivo answers it as you.

About noise

03 / 18

How do you stop one blog post raising an alarm?

A primary source.

A risk cannot move into the danger zone without at least one primary source.

Each risk sits on a radar of impact against likelihood. When a signal suggests a risk has moved, Vivo shows the new position as a ghost with a one-line reason. A person confirms the move or rejects it.

The top right of the radar is the danger zone. A move there needs a primary source. A secondary source on its own is not enough. Every confirmed move joins the trail with its signal, its date and the name of the person who confirmed it.

04 / 18

Won't we drown in news?

No. A person triages it.

The Radar searches for what your focus areas need, and a named person sorts what it finds.

The search brief for each focus area is its success criterion, the objectives under it and its risks. Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports on Monday and Thursday. A red-line exception interrupts that schedule.

Vivo makes the first pass on each signal. It names the criterion, says relevant or not and gives one line of reason. The person you delegate marks the signal relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Each dismissed signal teaches Vivo what counts as noise for your company.

A relevant signal that matches no known risk comes back as a question: Possible new risk?

05 / 18

Will we end up changing strategy every week?

No. Tactics move. Strategy holds.

Strategies fail in two ways. Inertia is doing nothing. Thrash is reacting to everything.

Dynamic Strategy works like steering a ship. You make small, early corrections, and once in a while you change course. Hold is a decision in its own right, and it marks the criterion as reviewed on that date. Adapt changes objectives and activities.

The wording of a criterion changes only through a Pivot, and a Pivot waits for someone with authority to confirm it. Leadership teams tell us the same thing: We're not changing strategy every week, but we are revising our tactics.

About the work

06 / 18

Is this another meeting?

It replaces one.

It replaces the strategy meeting that drifts.

A session in the Strategy Room runs 60 minutes by default. You choose weekly, fortnightly or monthly, and add a session when something can't wait.

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before, from what changed: new signals, risks entering the top right, parked questions coming back, carried-forward items, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible. Old business comes first. Each item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits the draft, and the room approves it as step one.

07 / 18

How do we stop the loudest voice setting the direction?

Sealed first takes.

Everyone records a position before the discussion starts. The positions are revealed together.

Each agenda item runs the same way: evidence, sealed first takes, a conversation with Vivo, a decision. Nobody anchors on the first opinion spoken because nobody has spoken yet.

In the conversation, Vivo can put the cost of doing nothing on the table, sketch scenarios, challenge an assumption, offer alternatives or raise a black swan. It answers the executive who says I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait.

08 / 18

What if we can't decide?

"Not yet" is a decision.

Each item ends in one of five decisions: Hold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage or Not yet.

Not yet opens with one question: what's missing? The item ends as Parked, with at least one trigger that brings it back. The trigger is new evidence, a person's view, a date or the next session. Or it ends as Let go, with a written rationale. It should feel like parking, not losing.

Nothing dangerous stays undiscussed. A risk that sits in the top right for 14 days with no decision lands on the session lead's Needs you list.

09 / 18

Will we find this decision in a year?

Yes. By its number.

Every decision gets a permanent number in the Record of Decisions.

At the end of each item, Vivo reads the entry back. Dissent goes in by name, with its reason. The session lead confirms the entry and it gets a number in the form SDS-2026-014.

A year later you can see what was decided, why, who disagreed and what happened next. Nobody has to rebuild the argument from slides.

10 / 18

Does Vivo listen in and take the minutes?

No.

Vivo does not listen to the room, and it writes no minutes.

The record holds decisions, not transcripts. Each entry is the decision, the reason and any dissent. Vivo reads it back and the session lead confirms it. Only confirmed entries go into the record.

About control

11 / 18

Who decides?

You do.

The human stays in the chair. Every change Vivo proposes waits for a person.

Strapivo has four roles: Owner, Admin, Member and Contributor. Contributors are the people responsible for activities. Before each session they report a status, one line on the result and an optional ask of leadership.

Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner. A decision beyond the team's authority goes to an approver in the app, as an exported memo or into the board pack. After the session, Strapivo prompts the lead to announce what changed and why.

12 / 18

Can I show the board that strategy moved between meetings?

Yes.

The radar records each move, and the board pack exports it.

The Tracking view shows where each risk moved since the last session, over 30 days or since origin. Each risk carries a trend word, Easing, Stable or Worsening, and the time it has held its position, such as top right · 27 days.

The board pack exports as Markdown. The plan exports as Markdown or CSV.

About fit

13 / 18

We already have a strategy from a consultant. Does it fit?

Yes.

Strapivo takes the strategy you have, in the form you have it.

Setup accepts a workshop report, a document, a pasted email or a plain prompt. No method is required. Vivo proposes where each item belongs, with prompts like This reads like an activity, not an objective. Move it down? An Owner accepts or changes each proposal.

The result is one Ambition for the portfolio, with optional targets such as turnover, margin and a year. Under it sit the success criteria, their objectives, the activities and the risks.

14 / 18

We have twelve priorities. Can we keep them all?

Keep them. Focus on four.

Fill a few glasses to the brim instead of all twelve a little.

A strategy usually carries 7 to 12 success criteria, each measured against the Ambition. You can flag up to four of them as focus areas. The Radar watches the world for those four.

Flag a fifth and Vivo asks whether you really want to. You can still say yes.

About what it is not

15 / 18

Is this a KPI dashboard?

No.

It is not a KPI dashboard, and it is not a to-do list.

Each success criterion carries today's light, green, yellow or red, and a person sets it. Under each criterion sit 2 to 4 objectives. Under each objective sit activities, and every activity has an action, a result, a timeframe and a person responsible. Its status is on track, at risk, off track or done.

The home screen is Mission Control. It shows the next session with a countdown, your Needs you list, the success criteria at a glance and recent decisions.

16 / 18

Does it sync with our task tools?

No. It exports.

Strapivo holds the strategy. Your task tools keep the tasks.

The plan exports as Markdown or CSV. Escalation memos export as Markdown or as copied text. Session invitations arrive by email as calendar files. There is no two-way sync, and we would rather say so in the first meeting than in the third.

About getting started

Humans decide and humans validate. Every recommendation Vivo makes traces back to the strategic memory your Owners approved.

How long until we run it ourselves?

Four to five hours.

We do the reading first.

You send what you have: the workshop report, the board paper, the email thread. We read it before we meet. Then we spend four to five hours with your team, and after that you run it.
