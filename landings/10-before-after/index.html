01 After the offsite

Before

The plan goes into a deck.

The team leaves with a strategy everyone agreed to and a deck that holds it. People go back to running the business, which is their job. The next full look happens at the annual review.

Before

A competitor's announcement lands in a few inboxes with "FYI" above it. Some people read it. Nobody ties it to the goal it affects, so it stays an interesting article.

03 Before the meeting

Before

The agenda comes from memory.

The chief of staff builds it from standing items and requests for time. It is a reasonable list. It reflects what people remember, and nobody checks it against what changed outside.

After

Vivo drafts it from what changed.

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session. It draws on relevant signals, risks entering the top right of the radar, parked questions that came back and activities at risk or off track. Every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits it, and the room approves it as step one.

  • Risk: price war in the core segment
    New business. Moved into the top right on Monday.

04 In the meeting

Before

The first opinion sets the frame.

The most senior or most confident person speaks first, and everyone else responds to that view. People with better information adjust their position before they share it.

After

Everyone commits before anyone talks.

For each item the room sees the evidence, then each person records a sealed first take. The takes are revealed together, and only then does the conversation start. Vivo can put the cost of doing nothing on the table, or argue the other side.

05 Making the call

Before

The outcome is an action item.

"Let's look into it" goes into the notes with a name next to it. Afterwards, nobody can say whether the strategy changed, stayed or was put off.

Action items
Look into pricing response. Owner: JM. Due: next meeting.

After

The outcome is one of five options.

Hold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage or Not yet. With Adapt, Vivo drafts the changes to objectives and activities. With Pivot, Vivo drafts new wording for the criterion, and it takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.

06 When the answer is "not yet"

Before

The question gets tabled.

It leaves the agenda and nobody owns bringing it back. Months later it returns as a new argument, and the team starts from the beginning.

After

The question gets parked with a trigger.

Not yet starts with "what's missing?" A Parked question has at least one trigger that brings it back: new evidence, a person's view, a date or the next session. If the team lets it go instead, it writes down why.

07 After the meeting

Before

Minutes go out.

Someone writes up the meeting and circulates it a few days later. The minutes list topics and action items. Disagreement rarely makes it onto the page.

After

A numbered record goes in.

Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions, with dissent by name and its reason. The session lead confirms it. The decision gets a permanent number.

08 Six months later

Before

Someone asks why.

The person who made the case has changed roles. The reasoning sits in a slide, an email thread or nowhere. A new analysis lands and the team restarts the old argument.

After

Someone opens the trail.

Each decision keeps its signals, its date, the person who confirmed it and the dissent. Each confirmed risk move sits on the same trail with its source. The team picks up the argument where it stopped.

09 When the board asks what moved

Before

The strategy team rebuilds the story.

A week of slide work reconstructs what changed since the last board meeting. It is accurate as far as memory goes.

What does not change.

Dynamic Strategy changes how often the team looks and what it records. These stay where they are.

Your method

Strapivo does not ask you to adopt a new framework. Setup takes a workshop report, a document, a pasted email or a plain prompt. Vivo proposes where each item belongs. A typical prompt reads "This reads like an activity, not an objective. Move it down?" An Owner accepts or rejects it.

Your people's authority

Vivo proposes; people decide. Vivo never changes a light. A risk moves only when a person confirms it, and a move into the danger zone needs at least one primary source. A Pivot waits for someone with authority.
