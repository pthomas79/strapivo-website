Briefing notes

Needs you · Read

Why the last strategy went into a drawer

Every other function got software. Strategy never did. A leadership team agrees its strategy in a workshop, and then the document goes into a drawer.

The market keeps moving. News arrives unconnected to what the team said must succeed. The gap shows at the quarterly review, when a correction already costs more.

"We finish the strategy, and then we forget it." What leadership teams tell us

Strapivo is the Strategic Design System. A brand design system has one logo, one type scale and one palette. Strapivo holds one business model, one set of principles and guardrails, and one answer to where you play and where you don't.

Dynamic Strategy works from that system. The team makes small, early corrections, and occasionally changes course. The strategy stays agreed. The tactics get revised.

Needs you · Risk

A risk has sat in the top right for 14 days

Every risk has a place on a radar of impact against likelihood. The top right is the danger zone.

The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area. Its search brief is the success criterion, its objectives and its risks. Each signal is dated, has a source, and is classed as primary or secondary.

A risk moves into the top right only on at least one primary source, "so that one blog post can't raise an alarm."

If a risk sits there for 14 days with no decision, it lands in the session lead's Needs you list. The Tracking view shows how each risk moved since the last session, with a trend word: Easing, Stable or Worsening.

Needs you · Proposed by Vivo

Confirm a ghost move

A new primary source suggests a supplier risk has become more likely. Vivo, the AI in Strapivo, doesn't move the risk. It places a ghost on the radar at the proposed position, with a one-line reason and the signal behind it.

You confirm or reject the move. A confirmed move joins the trail with its signal, its date and your name.

Vivo makes a first pass on every signal: which criterion it touches, whether it is relevant, and one line of reason. A delegated person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

Needs you · Trust

What Vivo is allowed to change

Vivo proposes; people decide. Vivo drafts and flags. People make every change.

Vivo never changes a light. When two focus activities go off track, it asks: "Light still green?" A Pivot that Vivo drafts takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it. Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner.

Every recommendation traces back to the strategic memory your team approved. Strapivo is hardwired to the Business Model Canvas. It is not a generic prompt with your PDF attached.

Strapivo runs on EU-only infrastructure, with GDPR by design.

Next session · Agenda

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before

The draft comes from what changed since the last session: relevant signals, risks entering the top right, parked questions that came back, carried-forward items, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible.

Old business comes first, then new business. Every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits the draft, and the room approves it as step one.

Sessions run 60 minutes by default, weekly, fortnightly or monthly, plus extra sessions on demand. Before each one, the people responsible for activities report a status, one line on the result and an optional ask of leadership.

Next session · First takes

First takes are sealed until everyone has one

For each agenda item, the room sees the evidence first. Then each person writes a first take, an individual position given before discussion. The takes are revealed together, "without the loudest voice anchoring us."

Then the conversation starts, with Vivo in it. Vivo can raise the cost of doing nothing, sketch scenarios, challenge an assumption, offer alternatives or name a black swan.

Success criteria

Eight criteria, four in focus

Each portfolio has one Ambition, with optional targets such as turnover, margin and a year. Seven to twelve success criteria sit under it. Each one carries today's light: green, yellow or red.

Up to four criteria are flagged as focus. "Fill a few glasses to the brim instead of all twelve a little." Flag a fifth and Strapivo asks: "You're diluting focus and creating noise. Do you really want to?"

Each criterion holds two to four objectives. Each activity sits under one objective, with an action, a result, a timeframe and a person responsible. The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area.

Decision · SDS-2026-014

Adapt keeps the criterion and changes the route

Adapt keeps the success criterion and changes how the team gets there. Vivo drafts the changes to objectives and activities. The team edits and accepts them.

In this example, two field-service activities moved to remote monitoring contracts. The head of service disagreed, and the record says so.

Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions, including dissent by name with its reason. The session lead confirms it. Each decision gets a permanent number, so anyone can find what was decided, why, and who disagreed.

Decision · SDS-2026-013

Hold is a decision too

Hold keeps the criterion as written. Strapivo marks it reviewed on that date.

A team can fail in two ways. Inertia means doing nothing. Thrash means reacting to everything. A dated Hold shows the board that the question was asked and answered.

Decision · SDS-2026-012

Not yet ends parked, with a trigger

Not yet starts with one question: what's missing? The answer ends one of two ways.

Parked means the question waits for at least one trigger that brings it back: new evidence, a person's view, a date or the next session. Let go means the team drops it and records the rationale.

Here, the second assembly site returns to the agenda when the supplier audit arrives. "So that it feels like parking, not losing."

Decision · SDS-2026-011

Take advantage, then escalate what's above you

Take advantage adds detour activities without changing the criterion. A competitor closed, so the team quotes for its customers.

Pivot is the fifth option. Vivo drafts revised wording for the criterion, and the change takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.

Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver in the app, as an exported memo, or into the board pack. The session lead is then prompted to announce what changed and why.
