# 16 · The ledger

Range: Confident (concept 16 of 40, ordered from conservative to a bit nuts)
Audience: Boards, chairs and anyone who has asked 'when did we decide that?'

## The concept

The page is a Record of Decisions. Its argument: a company that cannot find what it decided, why, and who disagreed does not have a strategy, it has a memory problem.

Structure: a short hero, then the ledger itself, a full-width register of about ten illustrative entries (SDS-2026-005 to SDS-2026-014) for one fictional company, labelled as an illustrative example. Columns: number, date, question, decision (one of the five options), dissent, status (in effect, awaiting Owner, parked until, let go, escalated to board). Rows expand to show the evidence, the first takes, the rationale, the dissent by name with its reason, and what happened next. One entry is 'Back from parking' with what is new since. Around the ledger, in margin columns, the copy explains what makes each field possible: the read-back, sealed first takes, parking triggers, escalation, Owner acceptance.

Signature element: the register. Mono numerals, ruled lines, tabular alignment, a filter by option, and a search field that filters the rows on the page. It should feel like a well-kept book of record, with nothing retro or skeuomorphic about it. On mobile each row becomes a stacked entry.
