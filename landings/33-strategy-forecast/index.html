01 · Observations

Where the readings come from.

Every reading starts as a signal. A signal is a dated observation with a source, marked primary or secondary. The Radar searches the outside world for each focus area. Its search brief is the success criterion, its objectives and its risks. Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports on Monday and Thursday.

Vivo, Strapivo's AI, makes the first pass. It names the criterion, says relevant or not, and gives one line of reason. A person you delegate marks each signal relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

When signals add up, Vivo proposes a new position for a risk. A person confirms or rejects it. Each confirmed move joins the trail with its signal, its date and the name of the person who confirmed it.

02 · Pressure

Pressure shows before the light changes.

A light turns red late. By then the cheap corrections are gone. So each risk carries two earlier readings.

The trend word says which way it is going: Easing, Stable or Worsening. Time held says how long it has stayed where it is, for example top right for 27 days.

A risk that sits in the top right for 14 days with no decision lands in the session lead's Needs you list. Nothing stays dangerous and undiscussed.

When two or more focus activities are off track, or a risk on that criterion enters the top right, Vivo asks: "Light still green?" Vivo never changes the light itself.

03 · Warnings

What counts as a warning.

A weather service grades its alerts so people know how seriously to take each one. Strapivo raises four kinds of flag, and each one has a rule behind it.

The strictest rule applies to the top right of the risk radar, the danger zone. A risk can only move there with at least one primary source, so that one blog post can't raise an alarm.

  • Advisory

    "Possible new risk?"

    A relevant signal that matches no risk the team has named.

  • Watch

    "Light still green?"

    Two or more focus activities are off track, or a risk on the criterion has entered the top right. The team decides the light.

  • Warning

    A risk enters the top right

    It needs a primary source and a person's confirmation. After 14 days with no decision, it goes to the session lead.

  • Exception

    A red line is crossed

    The red-line exception doesn't wait for Monday or Thursday. It interrupts the report schedule.

04 · The session

What the team does with a forecast.

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session, from what changed: new signals, risks entering the top right, parked questions coming back, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible. Old business comes first. Each item carries one line of reason.

The session lead edits the draft. The room approves it as the first item. Vivo proposes. People decide.

  1. 60 minOne session. Weekly, fortnightly or monthly, plus extra sessions on demand.
  2. Step 1Evidence first. The signals, the risk position and the trail behind it.
  3. Step 2Sealed first takes. Each person writes a position before discussion. Strapivo reveals them together, so nobody adjusts their view to the loudest voice.
  4. Step 3Conversation with Vivo. Ask for the cost of doing nothing, scenarios, a challenge, alternatives or a black swan.
  5. Step 4A decision. One of five.
  1. 01

    Hold

    Strapivo marks the criterion reviewed on that date.

  2. 02

    Adapt

    Vivo drafts changes to objectives and activities.

  3. 03

    Pivot

    Vivo drafts new wording for the criterion. It takes effect when someone with authority confirms it.

  4. 04

    Take advantage

    The team adds detour activities. The criterion stays as it is.

  5. 05

    Not yet

    Parked with a trigger that brings it back, or let go with a reason. It feels like parking, not losing.
