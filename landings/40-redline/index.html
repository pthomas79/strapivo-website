News arrives as noise, with no link to what the team said must succeed. Meetings anchor on the loudest voice. Decisions end up in slides and minutes that nobody reads. A year later, nobody can find what was decided, who disagreed, or what happened next.

§ 2 · Dynamic Strategy

Small corrections, made early.

Dynamic Strategy is how a leadership team keeps an agreed strategy honest between offsites. It avoids two failures. Inertia is doing nothing. Thrash is reacting to everything.

Strapivo tracks your goals and flags anything that goes off course. Each success criterion is measured against one Ambition and carries today's light: green, yellow or red. Vivo can ask "Light still green?" when focus activities slip. Vivo never changes the light itself.

Proposed by Vivo. Awaiting Owner.

"Goals" and "flags" are vague. Name the structure and say who sets the light.

See every goal at a glance in one powerful dashboard. Flag up to four success criteria as focus areas. Fill a few glasses to the brim instead of all twelve a little. Try to add a fifth and Strapivo asks: "You're diluting focus and creating noise. Do you really want to?"007

Under each criterion sit two to four objectives. Under each objective sit activities, each with an action, a result, a timeframe and a person responsible. Risks sit on a radar of impact against likelihood.

Bring a workshop report, a document or a pasted email. Vivo proposes where each item belongs: "This reads like an activity, not an objective. Move it down?" An Owner accepts or rejects it.

§ 3 · How it works

Between sessions, Vivo watches. In the session, people decide.

  1. Watch. The Radar reads over 4,000 sources a day for each focus area. Its search brief is the criterion, its objectives and its risks. Reports arrive on Monday and Thursday.
  2. Triage. Vivo assigns each signal to a criterion with a one-line reason. A named person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.
  1. Move. AI automatically updates your risk map as news breaks. Vivo proposes a new position for a risk as a ghost on the radar, with a one-line reason. A person confirms or rejects it. A move into the top right needs at least one primary source, so that one blog post can't raise an alarm.011

  1. Agenda. Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session from what changed. Old business comes first. The session lead edits it, and the room approves it as the first item.
  2. Session. Sixty minutes, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. For each item the room sees the evidence, gives first takes, talks it through with Vivo and decides.
  3. Record. Vivo reads back each decision, with dissent by name and its reason. The session lead confirms. The decision gets a permanent number.
  4. Announce. Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver. The lead is prompted to announce what changed and why.

§ 4 · The session

Every item ends in a decision. Waiting counts, if it has a trigger.

Everyone gets a say in the session. Before discussion, each person records a first take. The takes are revealed together, so the loudest voice does not anchor the room.

Proposed by Vivo. Awaiting Owner.

"Everyone gets a say" is a promise. Sealed first takes are the mechanism.

Vivo can raise the cost of doing nothing, a scenario, a challenge or an alternative. Then the room picks one of five decisions.

Hold
The criterion is marked reviewed on that date.
Adapt
Vivo drafts changes to objectives and activities.
Pivot
Vivo drafts new wording for the criterion. It takes effect when someone with authority confirms it.
Take advantage
Detour activities join the plan. The criterion stays as it is.
Not yet
The room asks what's missing. The question is parked with at least one trigger that brings it back, or let go with a reason. Parking it should not feel like losing it.
