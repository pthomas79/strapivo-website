Strategy

Strategy finally has a home

Every other function got software. Strategy never did. Strapivo keeps an agreed strategy current between offsites, and puts every decision on the record.

The Strategy Room. A leadership team agrees its strategy in a workshop. Then the strategy goes into a drawer. The world keeps moving, and nobody notices until the quarterly review, when a correction has become expensive.

Strapivo is the Strategic Design System. A brand design system gives a company one logo, one type scale and one palette for everything to plug into. Strapivo does the same for strategy. It holds the business model and its environment, the principles and guardrails for every call, and where the company plays and where it does not.

The working method is called Dynamic Strategy. Its makers compare it to steering a ship: small, early corrections, and occasionally a change of course. The team sets one Ambition, seven to twelve success criteria and up to four focus areas.

"Fill a few glasses to the brim instead of all twelve a little."

On choosing focus areas

Objectives sit under each criterion. Activities sit under objectives, each with an action, a result, a timeframe and a person responsible. Try to flag a fifth focus area and Vivo, the Strapivo AI, asks: "You're diluting focus and creating noise. Do you really want to?"

The backbone is the Business Model Canvas. Vivo works from the strategic memory the team approved, so it is hardwired to the business model. It is not a generic prompt with a PDF attached.

Watch

Radar reads the outside world against each focus area

Monday and Thursday. For each focus area, the Radar's search brief is the criterion, its objectives and its risks. Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports twice a week. A red-line exception interrupts that schedule.

Each signal is a dated observation with a source, classed primary or secondary. Vivo makes the first pass: which criterion, relevant or not, and one line of reason. A delegated person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

A relevant signal that matches no known risk arrives with a question: "Possible new risk?" The switch at the top of this page runs that pass on this front page.

Risk

One blog post cannot raise an alarm

The danger zone. Each risk sits on a radar of impact against likelihood. When a signal suggests a risk has moved, Vivo draws a ghost at the new position with a one-line reason. A person confirms or rejects it.

A move into the top right needs at least one primary source. Every confirmed move joins the trail with its signal, its date and the person who confirmed it.

Each risk carries a trend word, Easing, Stable or Worsening, and a time held, such as "top right · 27 days". A risk that stays in the top right for 14 days without a decision goes onto the session lead's Needs you list.

Session

Leadership meets for an hour and leaves with decisions

24 hours before. Vivo drafts the agenda from what changed. That means new signals, risks entering the top right, parked questions coming back, carried-forward items, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible. Old business comes first. Every item carries a one-line reason.

The session lead edits the draft. The room approves it as step one.

Sessions run 60 minutes by default, weekly, fortnightly or monthly, with extra sessions on demand. For each item the room sees the evidence first. Then everyone gives a sealed first take, a position stated before discussion. The takes are revealed together, so the loudest voice cannot anchor the room.

Then the room works the item with Vivo. It can lay out the cost of doing nothing, scenarios, a challenge, alternatives or a black swan.

Column

Help, not a co-author with the pen

The human stays in the chair

Leadership teams will not hand their strategy to a tool that rewrites it. In Strapivo, Vivo proposes and people decide.

Vivo never changes a light. When two or more focus activities are off track, or a risk on that criterion enters the top right, it asks: "Light still green?" A Pivot takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it. Every recommendation traces back to the strategic memory the team approved.

Vivo takes five roles. The Auditor asks where a number came from. The Advisor asks whether a move fits the principles. The Scout asks what moved against the plan. The Colleague thinks a question through with you. The Researcher goes and finds out.

The infrastructure runs in the EU only. GDPR is part of the design.

Record

Every decision gets a number

After the session. Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions, including any dissent, by name and with its reason. The session lead confirms. The entry gets a permanent number.

Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver in the app, as an exported memo, or into the board pack. Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner. The lead is then prompted to announce what changed and why.

Dynamic Strategy guards against two failure modes: inertia, which is doing nothing, and thrash, which is reacting to everything.
