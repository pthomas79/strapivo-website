The problem

A risk register is accurate on the day of the workshop.

Then nobody updates it. A supplier raises prices. A regulator publishes a draft. Nobody connects the news to the risks the team agreed on. The quarterly review finds it, after the cheap corrections have gone.

Strapivo is the Strategic Design System, one place where a company keeps its strategy current. The Radar is the part that watches the outside world. Between sessions, Vivo proposes small moves and a person signs off each one.

  • "I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait."
  • "Between offsites, nothing moves."

Calibration

Five rules decide what may move a risk.

A finance director will only trust a chart if they can see what put each point there. These rules apply to every risk on every radar in Strapivo.

Rule01

Every signal carries its source.

A signal is a dated observation with a source and a source class, primary or secondary. Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports on Monday and Thursday. A red-line exception interrupts that schedule.

Vivo makes the first pass and gives one line of reason. A person you delegate marks each signal relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

Rule03

A person confirms every move.

Vivo shows a proposed position as a ghost on the radar, with a one-line reason. Someone on your team confirms or rejects it. Each confirmed move joins the trail with its signal, its date and the name of the person who confirmed it.

Tracking replays that trail since the last session, over 30 days or since origin, like a radar shadow.

Rule04

After 14 days in the top right, a risk goes to the session lead.

A risk that sits in the top right for 14 days with no decision lands in the session lead's Needs you list, "so that nothing stays dangerous and undiscussed."

Rule05

A risk that enters the top right goes on the agenda.

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session from what changed. A risk entering the top right sits beside new signals, returning parked questions and activities at risk. Every item carries a one-line reason.

The session lead edits the draft. The room approves it as step one.

The human stays in the chair

Vivo proposes. People decide.

Vivo never moves a risk on its own. It never changes a success criterion's light. When two focus activities are off track or a risk on that criterion enters the top right, it asks one question: "Light still green?"

Every recommendation Vivo makes traces back to the strategic memory you approved. Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner.
