The ten questions

Answer each one yes or no for your company as it runs today.

  1. Can you name the last strategic decision your leadership team made, and the date it was made?
  2. Is there one place where that decision and its reasons are written down?
  3. If a named risk got worse this month, would it reach the next agenda without someone lobbying for it?
  4. When outside news arrives, does someone check it against the goals your strategy says must succeed?
  5. Do people state their position before the discussion starts?
  6. When someone disagrees with a decision, is their disagreement recorded with its reason?
  7. When you postpone a question, is there a trigger that brings it back?
  8. Does every strategic activity have one named person responsible and a timeframe?
  9. Do those people report a status to leadership before each strategy meeting?
  10. Could you show your board what changed in the strategy since its last meeting, and why?

If you skipped the check

Dynamic Strategy keeps an agreed strategy current between reviews.

Most strategies are agreed at an offsite and checked again at the annual review. The market moves in between. With Dynamic Strategy, a leadership team makes small corrections early, from evidence, and changes course when the evidence is strong enough. Strapivo is the software that runs it, and Vivo is its AI.

What the strategy holds

  • AmbitionOne per portfolio, with optional targets such as turnover, margin and a year.
  • Success criteria Usually 7 to 12. Each carries today's light, green, yellow or red.
  • Focus areasUp to 4 criteria. Vivo questions a fifth.
  • ObjectivesUsually 2 to 4 under each criterion.
  • Activities Q8An action, a result, a timeframe and one person responsible.
  • RisksPlaced on a radar of impact against likelihood.

The loop, and the questions it answers

  1. 01WatchThe Radar reads the outside world for each focus area, against its criterion, objectives and risks.Q4
  2. 02TriageVivo suggests which criterion a signal touches. A person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure.Q4
  3. 03MoveVivo proposes a new position for a risk. A person confirms it. A move into the danger zone needs a primary source.Q3
  4. 04AgendaVivo drafts the agenda 24 hours ahead from what changed. The room approves it as step one.Q3Q9
  5. 05SessionFor each item the room sees the evidence, gives sealed first takes, talks it through with Vivo and decides.Q5
  6. 06DecideHold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage or Not yet. Not yet ends as Parked with a trigger, or as Let go with a reason.Q7
  7. 07RecordVivo reads back each entry, dissent included, by name. The session lead confirms it.Q1Q2Q6
  8. 08EscalateDecisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver, as a memo or in the board pack.Q10

Each decision gets one entry and one number.

An entry in the Record of Decisions shows the decision, its date, the evidence behind it and who disagreed. It keeps its number for good, so you can cite it in a board pack a year later.

What leadership teams tell us

Leadership teams describe these gaps in their own words.

  • Behind Q1 and Q2"We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands."
  • Behind Q10"I can't show the board that strategy actually moved between meetings."
  • Behind Q3"I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait."
  • Behind Q8 and Q9"We finish the strategy, and then we forget it."

Dynamic Strategy guards against two failure modes. One is inertia, where nothing changes until the annual review. The other is thrash, where every headline changes the plan. The aim is tactics that change often and a strategy that changes when the evidence calls for it.
