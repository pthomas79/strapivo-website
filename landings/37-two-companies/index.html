Illustrative example · two fictional companies

Two companies got the same news on the same morning.

One made its first decision about it on day 4. The other found out what it meant on day 97. Follow both for 120 days, side by side.

A · Annual cycle

Aldmoor Packaging

Makes food trays and lids. Agrees its strategy at an offsite each January and reviews it every quarter.

B · Dynamic Strategy

Brenfield Packaging

Makes the same trays for the same customers. Runs Dynamic Strategy in Strapivo, with a 60-minute session every fortnight.

Day 0 · the signal

The regulator publishes a consultation paper. Food-contact trays must contain a minimum share of recycled plastic from 2028.

Responses close on day 90. Both companies use virgin resin today.

Aldmoor's people are good at their jobs. Petra in regulatory affairs spots the paper on the first morning. Aldmoor has no place for the news to go between reviews. Brenfield has one.

What made the difference

Brenfield had a practice for news that arrives between reviews.

Every other function got software. Strategy never did. Dynamic Strategy is how a leadership team makes small, early corrections to an agreed strategy, and occasionally changes course. These are the steps Brenfield took, in Strapivo.

  1. Day 0 to 1

    The Radar searches outside sources for each focus criterion.

    Its search brief is each focus criterion, with its objectives and risks. Vivo makes a first pass on every signal. A named person marks it Relevant, Irrelevant or Unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

  2. Day 2

    Risks move on a radar, and a person confirms each move.

    Vivo proposes the new position as a ghost, with a one-line reason. A move into the top right needs at least one primary source, so that one blog post can't raise an alarm.

  3. Day 3

    The agenda comes from what changed.

    Vivo drafts it 24 hours before the session. Old business first, then new. The session lead edits it and the room approves it.

  4. Day 4

    First takes are sealed, then revealed together.

    Each person commits before discussion starts. Nobody hears the loudest voice before giving their own view. The decision goes on the Record of Decisions with a permanent number, and dissent goes on it by name.

  5. Day 4 to 60

    Not yet means parked, with a trigger.

    A question that isn't ready is parked with at least one trigger to bring it back: evidence, a person's view, a date or the next session. The other ending is Let go, with a written rationale.

  6. Day 60

    A pivot waits for someone with authority.

    Vivo drafts the new wording. It takes effect only when an Owner confirms it.
