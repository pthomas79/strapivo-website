01  The problem

Most strategies go into a drawer after the offsite.

The world keeps moving. Nobody notices until the quarterly or annual review, when a correction has become expensive. News arrives as noise, with no link to what the team said must succeed.

  • 01
    "We finish the strategy, and then we forget it."
  • 02
    "We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands."
  • 03
    "I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait."
  • 04
    "I can't show the board that strategy actually moved between meetings."
  • 05
    "Between offsites, nothing moves."

Inertia

03  Dynamic Strategy

Eight steps between one session and the next.

Dynamic Strategy keeps an agreed strategy current. It works like steering a ship. You make small, early corrections, and occasionally you change course. The loop runs on the structure you already agreed: one Ambition, seven to twelve success criteria, up to four of them in focus, and the objectives, activities and risks beneath them.

  1. 01

    Watch

    The Radar reads the outside world for each focus area. Its search brief is the success criterion, with its objectives and risks. Each signal is dated and carries its source.

  2. 02

    Triage

    Vivo makes a first pass and gives a one-line reason. A person you delegate marks each signal relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.

  3. 03

    Move

    Vivo proposes a new position for a risk on the risk radar. A person confirms or rejects it. A move into the danger zone needs at least one primary source.

  4. 04

    Agenda

    Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session, from what changed. Old business comes first. The session lead edits it, and the room approves it as the first item.

  5. 05

    Session

    Each item starts with the evidence. Everyone gives a first take before the discussion, and the takes are revealed together. The first person to speak no longer sets the position for everyone else.

  6. 06

    Decide

    The room picks one of five options. Vivo drafts the changes that follow. A pivot takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.

    HoldAdaptPivotTake advantageNot yet

  7. 07

    Record

    Vivo reads back each entry, with any dissent by name and its reason. The session lead confirms it. Each decision gets a permanent number, such as SDS-2026-014.

  8. 08

    Announce

    Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver or into the board pack. Strapivo then prompts the lead to tell the organisation what changed and why.

04  Trust

The human stays in the chair.

Leadership teams ask for "help, not a co-author with the pen." Vivo proposes. People decide. Every recommendation traces back to the strategic memory your team approved.

  • Vivo never changes a light.

    When two or more focus activities are off track, Vivo asks "Light still green?" The answer comes from a person.

  • One blog post can't raise an alarm.

    A risk moves into the danger zone only with at least one primary source and a person's confirmation.

  • Nothing dangerous stays undiscussed.

    A risk that sits in the danger zone for 14 days with no decision goes onto the session lead's "Needs you" list.

  • Accepted truth has an Owner.

    Changes to the strategic memory wait for an Owner to accept them. A revised success criterion waits for someone with authority.

  • Strapivo keeps dissent on record.

    The Record of Decisions names who disagreed and why. A year later, you can still find what was decided and on what evidence.
