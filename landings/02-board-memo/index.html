Summary

We agree our strategy once a year and then leave it alone until the next review. This paper proposes that the leadership team adopt Dynamic Strategy, a standing routine that checks the agreed strategy against the outside world every few weeks, and run it in Strapivo. The board would receive a numbered record of every decision, with dissent named, and a board pack that shows how the strategy moved between meetings. No change to the strategy takes effect until a named person confirms it.

1Purpose

1.1

This paper asks the board to approve a change in how the leadership team keeps the strategy current between formal reviews. It covers the problem, the proposed routine, its controls and the risks.

2Background

2.1

We agree the strategy at an offsite, write it up and file it. The market keeps moving. We notice at the quarterly or annual review, when a correction costs more than it would have a few months earlier.

2.2

News reaches us as noise. Nobody ties it to the goals we said must succeed, so nobody can say whether a given report matters.

2.3

In meetings, the loudest voice wins. Decisions end up in slides and minutes nobody reads. A year later we cannot find what was decided, why, or who disagreed.

2.4

Other leadership teams describe the same problem in the same words.

"We finish the strategy, and then we forget it."

"I can't show the board that strategy actually moved between meetings."

Unattributed. Said to Strapivo by leadership teams.

3What changes

3.1

The team writes the agreed strategy down once, in a fixed structure. One Ambition sits at the top. Beneath it sit 7 to 12 success criteria, each with a green, yellow or red light. Up to four criteria are marked as focus. Each criterion holds a few objectives, and each objective holds activities with a result, a timeframe and a named person.

A fifth focus area triggers a warning. Strapivo keeps focus narrow on purpose.

3.2

Risks sit on a radar of impact against likelihood. The top right of that radar is the danger zone.

3.3

Strapivo then runs a standing loop around this structure. Vivo, Strapivo's AI, reads and drafts. People decide. Each turn of the loop has these steps:

  1. WatchStrapivo's market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day against each focus area and reports on Monday and Thursday. Every signal carries a date and a source.
  2. TriageVivo suggests which criterion a signal belongs to, with a one-line reason. A named person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure.
  3. MoveVivo proposes a new position for a risk. A person confirms or rejects it. A move into the danger zone needs at least one primary source, so one blog post cannot raise an alarm. See Exhibit A.
  4. AgendaVivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session from what changed. The session lead edits it. The room approves it as the first item.
  5. SessionA 60-minute meeting, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. Each member records a first take before discussion. The takes are revealed together, so the first speaker does not set the answer.
  6. DecideEach item ends in Hold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage or Not yet. A "Not yet" is parked with a trigger that brings it back, or let go with a reason.
  7. RecordVivo reads each decision back. The session lead confirms it. It gets a permanent number and enters the Record of Decisions. See Exhibit B.

The routine allows five decisions. There is no sixth.

4Governance

4.1

Who decides. Owners hold the accepted strategy, and any change to it waits for an Owner. The session lead runs each session and confirms each entry in the record.

Roles: Owner, Admin, Member, Contributor.

4.2

What the AI may do. Vivo sorts signals, proposes risk positions, drafts the agenda and drafts changes to objectives and activities. Every recommendation traces back to the strategy the team approved.

4.3

What the AI may not do. Vivo does not decide. It does not change a criterion's light. It does not move a risk without a person's confirmation. A Pivot changes the wording of a criterion only when someone with authority confirms it.

Vivo drafts. A person signs off every change.

4.4

Nothing left undiscussed. A risk that sits in the danger zone for 14 days without a decision goes onto the session lead's "Needs you" list.

4.5

Escalation. Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver in the app, as an exported memo, or into the board pack.

4.6

What the board receives. The board gets a Markdown board pack and the Record of Decisions. Each entry shows the decision, the evidence, the date, who confirmed it, and any dissent by name with its reason.

The plan also exports as CSV.

4.7

Data. Strapivo runs on infrastructure inside the EU and is designed for GDPR.

5Risks

5.1

There are two ways to fail. Inertia means doing nothing until the annual review. Thrash means reacting to every headline. The routine has a control for each.

"We're not changing strategy every week, but we are revising our tactics."

5.2
Risks and controls
RiskControl
Of not adoptingThe strategy stays filed. We see threats at the annual review and cannot say what waiting cost us.
ThrashA danger-zone move needs a primary source. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise. Hold is a recorded decision, so a choice to change nothing is on the record.
AI overreachVivo proposes and drafts. People confirm every move, every decision and every change to the strategy. See 4.3.
TimeStrapivo does the reading first. Setup takes four to five hours. After that the team runs it.

6Recommendation

6.1

That the leadership team adopt Dynamic Strategy as its standing routine between strategy reviews, and run it in Strapivo.

6.2

That sessions run fortnightly for the first two quarters, after which the board reviews the Record of Decisions and sets the cadence.

Sessions can run weekly, fortnightly or monthly, with extra sessions on demand.

6.3

That the board receive the board pack and the Record of Decisions with each regular board paper.
