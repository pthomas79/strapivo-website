The Strategy Room · one session, minute by minute

Sixty minutes that end in decisions.

Most strategy meetings have an agenda and no shape. A session in Strapivo's Strategy Room has both. Scroll, and the clock runs through one session of Dynamic Strategy in the order it happens.

24 hours before the session

Vivo drafts the agenda the day before.

Vivo is Strapivo's AI. A day before the session it reads what changed since the last one. That covers new signals, risks that entered the top right, parked questions whose trigger fired, carried-forward items, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible.

Old business goes first. Every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits the draft.

Contributors report as well. Each person responsible for an activity gives a status, one line on the result and, if needed, an ask of leadership.

09 to 14 min

The first new item opens with its evidence.

The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area. On Monday it logged signals against recurring service revenue. Vivo proposed moving one risk into the top right, the danger zone. A person confirmed the move.

A move into the top right needs at least one primary source, so that one blog post can't raise an alarm. The room sees each signal with its date and source before anyone gives an opinion.

14 to 17 min

Everyone commits before anyone speaks.

Each person gives a first take alone, as a position and one line of reason. The takes stay sealed until all of them are in. Then the room sees them together.

Disagreement shows up while it can still change the outcome. Nobody can sway a position that was written before they spoke.

17 to 28 min

Then the room asks what waiting costs.

The conversation happens between the people in the room, and with Vivo when they ask. Here the lead asks Vivo for the cost of doing nothing. Vivo answers from the strategic memory the team approved and the signals on file, and names each source it used.

This item ran one minute past its time guide. The clock turned warm to tell the lead. The time guide stops nobody, and the lead let the room finish the point.

28 to 32 min

The item ends in one of five decisions.

The room picks one of five options. It cannot leave with "let's discuss it again". If the answer is Not yet, the room names what is missing.

Here the room chose Adapt. Vivo drafts changes to the objectives and activities under recurring service revenue. The room edits the draft before it stands.

32 to 43 min

The second item takes half the time.

Vivo asks "Light still green?" when two or more focus activities on a criterion are off track. Two are. Vivo never changes the light itself.

The room reads the contributors' reports, gives first takes and sets the light to yellow. The decision is Not yet. The missing piece is Lena's hiring plan. The room parks the question with that plan as its trigger, and it returns when the plan arrives.

43 to 46 min

What the room did not reach moves forward.

Lena's ask for a second field technician did not get its turn. It moves to the next agenda as old business, with its reason attached. The session lead decides this here, in the room.

A risk can also wait too long. If one sits in the top right for 14 days with no decision, it lands in the session lead's Needs you list. Nothing stays dangerous and undiscussed.

46 to 55 min

Vivo reads the record back, dissent included.

Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions. Each entry says what was decided and why. It names who disagreed and gives their reason. The session lead confirms the entry and it gets a permanent number.

A year from now, someone will ask why the team chose this. The answer will be in the record, with its number.

55 to 60 min

The hour ends on time.

The room leaves with three numbered decisions. One of them is a parked question with its trigger. One more item moves to the next session.

Some decisions go beyond the team's authority. Those go to an approver in the app, as an exported memo, or into the board pack. Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner. Strapivo then prompts the lead to announce what changed and why.

After minute 60

Between sessions, the Radar keeps watching.

  • Radar

    Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports on Monday and Thursday. A red-line exception interrupts that schedule.

  • Triage

    Vivo makes a first pass on each signal. A delegated person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Vivo uses dismissed signals to learn what counts as noise.

  • Trail

    Every confirmed risk move joins the trail with its signal, its date and the person who confirmed it. The board can see the strategy move between meetings.

  • Next agenda

    Vivo starts the next draft from what changed. The cycle runs weekly, fortnightly or monthly, and extra sessions run on demand.

Who holds the pen

Vivo proposes. People decide.

  • Moves

    Vivo proposes a new risk position as a ghost on the radar, with a one-line reason. A person confirms or rejects it.

  • Lights

    Vivo asks whether a light is still green. Only the room changes it.

  • Criteria

    A Pivot takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.

  • Data

    Every recommendation traces back to the strategic memory you approved. EU-only infrastructure. GDPR by design.

Setup takes an afternoon.

We do the reading first. Bring a workshop report, a strategy document or a pasted email. Vivo proposes where each item belongs: ambition, success criterion, objective, activity or risk. Owners accept or move each proposal.
