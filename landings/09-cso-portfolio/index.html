01 · The gap

You review the plan once a year. The market moves every week.

Your team agrees the strategy at the offsite. Then nobody opens it until the next review. Signals arrive as news, unconnected to what the team said must succeed. By the quarterly review, a cheap correction has become an expensive one.

What leadership teams tell us

  • "Between offsites, nothing moves."
  • "We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands."
  • "I can't show the board that strategy actually moved between meetings."
  • "I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait."

02 · The object model

One Ambition per portfolio. Everything else is read against it.

Each portfolio has one Ambition and usually 7 to 12 success criteria. A criterion applies to named business models. You flag up to four criteria as focus areas. Try a fifth and Vivo asks, "You're diluting focus and creating noise. Do you really want to?"

Risks carry a scope. A risk belongs to the whole portfolio or to one or more business models. The product says objective, activity and result. It never says KPI or OKR.

03 · The radar

Read the risks of one business model, or of the whole portfolio.

The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area. Its search brief is the criterion, its objectives and its risks. Market intelligence reads over 4,000 sources a day and reports on Monday and Thursday. A red-line exception interrupts that schedule.

Vivo makes the first pass on each signal, with one line of reason. A delegated person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise. A move into the top right needs at least one primary source, so a single blog post can't put a risk there.

04 · The session agenda

Vivo drafts the agenda from what changed, 24 hours ahead.

Vivo assembles the draft from new signals, risks entering the top right, returning parked questions, carried-forward items, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible. Old business comes first. The session lead edits the draft and the room approves it as step one.

On each item, people give sealed first takes before discussion. The takes are revealed together, so no one adjusts their view to the loudest voice.

05 · Decisions

Five options, each with a defined consequence.

OptionWhat changes in the plan
HoldStrapivo marks the criterion reviewed on that date.
AdaptVivo drafts changes to objectives and activities.
PivotVivo drafts new wording for the criterion. It takes effect when someone with authority confirms it.

Not yetThe team asks what's missing. The item ends as Parked, with at least one trigger that brings it back, or as Let go, with a rationale.

What the team can't decide goes to the person who can.

07 · Execution reports back

The people responsible for activities report status themselves.

In Strapivo these people are Contributors. Before each session they report a status, one line on the result and an optional ask of leadership. Their reports feed the agenda.

When two or more focus activities are off track, Vivo asks, "Light still green?" Vivo never changes the light itself. Strapivo is not a to-do list and not a KPI dashboard.

08 · Setup

Bring the strategy you have. It sits on the Business Model Canvas.

Setup accepts the output of any strategy method. Strapivo holds each business model as a Business Model Canvas, and Vivo works from those canvases. It is not a generic prompt with your PDF attached.

09 · Who decides

The human stays in the chair.

Vivo proposes. People decide.

Every move, placement and draft needs a person to confirm it. Every recommendation traces back to the strategic memory you approved.

Four roles

Owner, Admin, Member and Contributor. Owners approve changes to accepted truth. Contributors report on their activities.
