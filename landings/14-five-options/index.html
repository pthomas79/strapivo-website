Most strategy meetings end with "let's keep an eye on it".

Nobody decided to wait, so nobody owns the waiting. The risk returns at the quarterly review, larger. The argument starts again, because nobody wrote down who disagreed.

A meeting produces a decision when everyone knows the possible answers before it starts. Strapivo names five, and every item on the agenda ends on one of them.

What leadership teams tell us

  • "We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands."
  • "I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait."
  • "Between offsites, nothing moves."

The five answers, and what happens after each one.

01

Hold

The criterion still holds. Keep the plan. Holding is a decision, and it goes on the record like any other.

Vivo drafts
Nothing to change. Strapivo marks the criterion reviewed on that date.
Who confirms
The session lead confirms the record entry.
Example
Freight costs rose for a third month, but margin is still on target. Hold.

Adapt

Vivo drafts
Changes to objectives and activities.
Who confirms
Owners accept, edit or reject each change.
Example
A key supplier missed two deliveries. Add an activity to qualify a second supplier.
03

Pivot

The criterion itself changes. Strapivo asks for the most authority on this answer.

Vivo drafts
Revised wording for the criterion.
Who confirms
Someone with authority. Until then, the old wording stays in force.
Example
New regulation closes one market. Rewrite the growth criterion around the two that remain.
04

Take advantage

Something moved in your favour. Act on it without changing the criterion.

Vivo drafts
Detour activities, added beside the existing plan.
Who confirms
Owners accept the activities. The criterion stays as written.
Example
A rival leaves a region. Add an activity to approach its customers this quarter.
05

Not yet

The room cannot decide today and records why. Not yet always ends in Parked or Let go, so every question gets an outcome.

Vivo asks one question: "What's missing?"

Ending one

Parked

The question waits with at least one trigger. When a trigger fires, the question returns to the agenda as old business.

  • New evidence
  • A person's view
  • A date
  • The next session
Ending two

Let go

The room drops the question and records why. Anyone who raises it again reads that rationale first.

The two endings exist "so that it feels like parking, not losing."

Who confirms
The session lead confirms the entry, with its trigger or its rationale.
Example
An offer to buy a small competitor. Parked until the due diligence summary arrives.
Before the answer

How a question gets onto the agenda.

The five answers only help if the right questions arrive, in the right order.

A

The Radar

The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area. Vivo sorts each signal first. A delegated person marks it relevant, irrelevant or unsure.

Vivo proposes risk moves with a one-line reason, and a person confirms them. A move into the top right needs at least one primary source, so one blog post cannot raise an alarm.

Risk radar · NowFocus · Nordic mid-market
B

The agenda

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session from what changed since the last one.

Old business comes first, and every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits the draft. The room approves it as step one.

C

Sealed first takes

Each person records a first take before discussion. Strapivo reveals them together, so nobody adjusts their view to the loudest voice.

Then the room reviews the evidence, talks it through with Vivo and picks one of the five answers. Vivo can raise the cost of doing nothing, a scenario or a challenge.
