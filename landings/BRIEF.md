# Shared brief for all Strapivo landing page concepts

Every concept folder in `landings/` holds a `prompt.md` (the concept) and an `index.html` (the result). This file is the shared input: facts, brand, build rules. The concept prompt decides layout, angle and tone. This brief decides what is true and what the brand allows.

## 1. What Strapivo is

Strapivo is the Strategic Design System: one place where a company's strategy lives, stays current and gets used. Sources: sales decks to 27 Oct 2026, brand stylesheet 6 Nov 2026, product spec 18 Nov 2026.

- Core line: "Strategy finally has a home."
- Hook: "Every other function got software. Strategy never did."
- Slogan: "Any AI can answer a question. Only Strapivo answers it as you."
- Principle: "The human stays in the chair." and "Vivo proposes; people decide."
- Analogy: a brand design system has one logo, one type scale and one palette that everything plugs into. Strapivo does that for strategy: the business model and its environment, the principles and guardrails for every call, where you play and where you don't.
- The formula: Strategic memory (what's true inside your company) + Market intelligence (live signals from outside it) + Strategic session (the conversation where the two meet) = Lasting advantage.
- Backbone: the Business Model Canvas. "Hardwired to the business model. Not a generic prompt with your PDF attached."
- Vivo is the name of Strapivo's AI.
- The Strategy Room is where a leadership team runs its strategy in Strapivo.

## 2. The emphasis: Dynamic Strategy

Every page leads with or builds toward Dynamic Strategy. It is how a leadership team keeps an agreed strategy honest and evolving, "like steering a ship: small, early corrections, occasionally a change of course."

### The problem it answers

- A strategy is agreed in a workshop and then "goes into a drawer". The world keeps moving and nobody notices until a quarterly or annual review, when it is too late to course-correct cheaply.
- News arrives as noise, unconnected to what the team said must succeed.
- Meetings drift and anchor on the loudest voice. Decisions vanish into slides and minutes nobody reads.
- Later, nobody can find what was decided, why, who disagreed, or what happened next.
- AI tools that rewrite strategy can't be trusted with it. Teams want "help, not a co-author with the pen."
- Executive voices from the decks: "We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands." "I can't show the board that strategy actually moved between meetings." "I know there's a threat coming. I don't know what it costs us to wait." "We finish the strategy, and then we forget it." "Between offsites, nothing moves."
- Two failure modes: inertia (doing nothing) and thrash (reacting to everything). "We're not changing strategy every week, but we are revising our tactics."

### The structure

- Ambition: one per portfolio, with optional measurable targets such as turnover, margin and a year. Everything is read against it.
- Success criteria: usually 7 to 12 goals measured against the Ambition. Each carries today's light: green, yellow or red.
- Focus areas: up to 4 success criteria flagged as focus. "Fill a few glasses to the brim instead of all twelve a little." Trying to add more gets the prompt: "You're diluting focus and creating noise. Do you really want to?"
- Objectives: usually 2 to 4 under each success criterion.
- Activities: each sits under one objective and has an action, a result, a timeframe and a person responsible. Status: on track, at risk, off track, done.
- Risks: each has a position on a radar of impact against likelihood, and a scope (the whole portfolio, or one or more business models).
- Product vocabulary: objective, activity, result. The product never says KPI, OKR or key result. It is not a to-do list and not a KPI dashboard.
- Method-agnostic: setup accepts a workshop report, a document, a pasted email or a plain prompt. Vivo proposes where each item belongs ("This reads like an activity, not an objective. Move it down?") and Owners accept it.

### The loop

1. Watch. The Radar watches the outside world for each focus area. Its search brief is the criterion, its objectives and its risks. Signals are dated observations with a source and a source class, primary or secondary.
2. Triage. Vivo makes a first pass: which criterion, relevant or not, one line of reason. A delegated person marks each signal relevant, irrelevant or unsure. Signal states: New, Relevant, Watching, Dismissed. Dismissed signals teach Vivo what counts as noise.
3. Move. Vivo proposes a new position for a risk as a ghost on the radar with a one-line reason. A person confirms or rejects. A move into the top right, the danger zone, needs at least one primary source, "so that one blog post can't raise an alarm." Every confirmed move joins the trail with its signal, date and confirmer.
4. Agenda. Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours before the session from what changed: signals, risks entering the top right, returning parked questions, carried-forward items, activities at risk or off track, and asks from the people responsible. Old business first, then new business. Every item carries a one-line reason. The session lead edits it and the room approves it as step one.
5. Session. 60 minutes by default, weekly, fortnightly or monthly, plus extra sessions on demand. Per item: evidence, sealed first takes, conversation with Vivo, decision. First takes are individual positions given before discussion and revealed together, "without the loudest voice anchoring us." Vivo can prompt on the cost of doing nothing, scenarios, a challenge, alternatives, a black swan.
6. Decide. Five options: Hold, Adapt, Pivot, Take advantage, Not yet.
   - Hold: the criterion is marked reviewed on that date.
   - Adapt: Vivo drafts changes to objectives and activities.
   - Pivot: Vivo drafts revised wording for the criterion. It takes effect only when someone with authority confirms it.
   - Take advantage: detour activities are added without changing the criterion.
   - Not yet: the question is "what's missing?" It ends as Parked, with at least one trigger that brings it back (evidence, a person's view, a date, the next session), or as Let go, with a rationale. "So that it feels like parking, not losing."
7. Record. Vivo reads back each entry for the Record of Decisions, including dissent by name with its reason. The session lead confirms. Each decision gets a permanent number such as SDS-2026-014.
8. Escalate and announce. Decisions beyond the team's authority go to an approver in the app, as an exported memo, or into the board pack. Changes to accepted truth wait for an Owner. The lead is prompted to announce what changed and why.

### Details worth showing

- Radar views: Now and Tracking. Tracking shows movement since the last session, over 30 days or since origin, "like a radar shadow."
- Trend word per risk: Easing, Stable or Worsening. Time held: for example "top right · 27 days".
- No-decision nudge: a risk that sits in the top right for 14 days with no decision lands in the session lead's "Needs you" list, "so that nothing stays dangerous and undiscussed."
- "Light still green?" Vivo asks when two or more focus activities are off track or a risk on that criterion enters the top right. Vivo never changes the light itself.
- "Possible new risk?": a relevant signal that matches no known risk.
- Mission Control, the home screen: next session with countdown, Needs you, success criteria at a glance, recent decisions. "Everything that matters. One screen."
- Roles: Owner, Admin, Member, Contributor. Contributors are the people responsible for activities. Before each session they report a status, one line on the result and an optional ask of leadership.
- Exports: the plan and the board pack as Markdown, the plan as CSV, escalation memos as Markdown or copied text, calendar files by email.
- Market intelligence reads "over 4,000 sources a day" and reports on Monday and Thursday. A red-line exception interrupts that schedule.
- Five roles Vivo can take: Auditor ("Where did this number come from?"), Advisor ("Does this fit our principles?"), Scout ("What moved against the plan?"), Colleague ("Think this through with me."), Researcher.
- Lenses apply the perspective of a specific strategy practitioner to a question.
- Sample prompts from the decks: "Will my business survive AI?" and "Roast my business model."
- Trust: "Humans decide. Humans validate. Every recommendation the AI makes traces back to the strategic memory you approved." "EU-only infrastructure. GDPR by design."
- Onboarding: "We do the reading first." "Four to five hours, and then you run it."

## 3. Audience

Clients are SMEs and corporates. Readers are a CEO or managing director, a chief strategy officer and the strategy team, general managers and business unit leaders, board members. They are senior, time-poor and sceptical of AI claims. Write for them: direct, concrete, calm. Wit is welcome when it is dry. Nothing cute, no gamer or startup-bro register, no mascots.

## 4. Truth rules

- Do not invent customers, logos, testimonials, awards, statistics, percentages, ROI figures or press quotes. There are no public case studies.
- Do not name any real company as a customer or prospect.
- Worked examples are welcome. Use a fictional company and label the block "Illustrative example". Decision numbers follow the form SDS-2026-014.
- Quotes in section 2 are voices of typical executives. Present them unattributed or as "what leadership teams tell us". Never attach a name or a company.
- Do not state prices. Do not offer a free trial, a demo sandbox or self-serve sign-up.
- Do not promise these (not built): AI meeting minutes, Vivo listening to the room, calendar integration, two-way sync with task tools, KPI dashboards, voting or quorum rules, a mobile app, audio briefings, Teams integration.

Wilkens (CCO and co-founder), Paul Cubbon (co-founder and advisor), Dr. Alex Osterwalder (co-founder and advisor, creator of the Business Model Canvas). Add no biography beyond these roles.
- Spell out "Dynamic Strategy". Do not use the acronym SDS in copy except inside decision numbers.
- If the Business Model Canvas is drawn, include the credit line "Business Model Canvas adapted from Strategyzer.com, licensed under CC BY-SA 3.0."

No other typeface, no italics from other families, no weight above 500. Hierarchy comes from size, not weight. Headings use letter-spacing around -0.015em to -0.03em.

Logo. The mark is a plain ring, the "O-mark": `<circle cx="50" cy="50" r="42" fill="none" stroke="#0082E6" stroke-width="9"/>` in a 100 by 100 viewBox. Brand don'ts. No neon cyber look. No generic blue-gradient SaaS template. No purple gradients, glassmorphism, blobs, emoji, stock photos, AI-generated imagery or 3D renders. No fake logo wall. No row of three icon cards as a default section. Radii stay small, 2 to 8 px. Shadows are rare and soft.

## 7. Build rules

- Deliver one file: `index.html` in your concept folder. CSS and JavaScript inline. Vanilla only: no frameworks, no CDN libraries, no build step. The only network request is the JetBrains Mono stylesheet.
- All graphics are hand-built: inline SVG, CSS, or canvas. The product has no screenshots you may use, so draw interface fragments yourself in HTML and CSS when you need them.
- Works from `file://` and from a static server. Reference shared files only as `../../fonts/...` and `../../assets/logo/...`.
- Responsive from 360 px to 1920 px wide. No horizontal page scroll unless your concept asks for it, and then give it a sound small-screen fallback.
- Accessible: semantic landmarks, one `h1`, logical heading order, visible focus states, keyboard access to every interactive element, alt text or `aria-hidden` on graphics, AA contrast. Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The page still reads if JavaScript fails: content is in the HTML, scripts add behaviour.
- Motion is purposeful and slow. Nothing bounces, nothing autoplays sound, nothing hijacks scroll speed.
- `<title>` and meta description are specific to your concept. Add `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`.
- A real landing page, complete: header, the argument, how it works, the trust part, a closing call to action, footer. Roughly 500 to 1100 words of visible copy unless your concept says otherwise. No lorem ipsum, no "TODO".
- Stay in your folder. Do not edit anything else in the repo. Do not commit. Do not start subagents.

## 8. Copy rules

- British English. Sentence case headlines. No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in visible copy. Straight quotes.
- Short declarative sentences, concrete nouns. One thought per headline. No headline built from marketing adjectives.
- Say what the product does. Name the mechanism. Cut any sentence that could sit unchanged on another company's site.
