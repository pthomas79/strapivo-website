Vague deferral

Why "let's revisit this later" breaks strategy

Leadership teams say this when evidence is missing or the room is split. Without a named return trigger or a written close, deferred questions fail in two ways.

"We restart the same argument every time a new analysis lands."

What leadership teams tell us

01 · Dropped

It disappears into old minutes

Nobody owns the missing evidence. The market shifts while the question sits untouched until the next annual offsite.

02 · Repeated debate

It returns uninvited

Three months later, a fresh slide deck lands. The room spends another hour debating the same point from memory.

Formal deferment

Two clean exits from "Not yet"

In Strapivo, deferring a question requires a formal call. When the room is not ready to decide, leaders answer "What is missing?" and pick one of two exits, so that it feels like parking, not losing.

The 60-minute loop

All five options in one weekly session

Parking works because it runs inside a weekly 60-minute session. Every question arrives with its evidence and ends with one of five recorded calls.

01 · Radar watches

The Radar tracks outside signals against your agreed focus areas and flags risks that move.

02 · 24-hour agenda

Vivo drafts the agenda 24 hours ahead. Old business comes first, including parked items whose trigger is met.

03 · Sealed first takes

Each leader submits a view before discussion. Strapivo reveals all views together so the loudest voice cannot anchor the room.
