# 57. Primary source required

- Range: Third round: landing page candidates
- Audience: Chief strategy officers and strategy teams tired of noisy alerts
- Colours: House palette, plus muted steel (#7A8591) for dismissed secondary noise and deep teal (#0F766E) for verified primary sources.

## The task

Build a landing page for Strapivo as a single `index.html` in this folder. It is one of twenty third-round concepts. Each is meant to work as Strapivo's real main landing page, and each must look and read differently from the other 19 and from the 50 pages of rounds one and two.

## The concept

Built on one strict product rule from the brief: a move into the top right of the radar (the danger zone) requires at least one primary source, "so that one blog post can't raise an alarm." Strategy teams reject monitoring tools because they flood inboxes with recycled commentary. Strapivo reads over 4,000 sources a day against your focus areas, separates primary sources from secondary commentary, learns from every dismissal, and asks a person to confirm before any risk moves.

(Note: this is one of ONLY TWO pages in round three explicitly authorized to use "over 4,000 sources a day".)

**Hero.** Balanced two-column desktop hero. Left column: headline "One blog post cannot raise an alarm." Two plain sentences explaining Strapivo: the Strategic Design System that filters live market signals against your agreed focus areas and brings only verified, primary-source shifts to your leadership team's weekly session. Primary call to action. Right column: Drawing 1, a clean architectural **two-stage signal gate** (labelled "Illustrative example"). On the left of the gate, three secondary commentary items (trade blog, opinion column, recycled summary) stop cleanly at a thin rule marked "Secondary: held or dismissed". Passing cleanly through the teal-marked "Primary source" slot is one dated regulatory filing, which proposes a single ghost move on the radar for human confirmation.

**The page.** At most three product drawings total. Sections in order:
1. Why alerts fail leadership teams. News arrives as noise, unconnected to what the team said must succeed. Without a search brief tied to your success criteria and risks, monitoring creates work instead of clarity.
2. Three filters before anything reaches the room. Drawing 2: a clean horizontal three-step strip (Search brief tied to up to 4 focus areas -> Delegated triage marking Relevant, Watching or Dismissed, teaching Vivo what counts as noise -> Primary source + human confirmation to enter the danger zone).
3. From verified signal to recorded decision. Monday and Thursday reports (with red-line exceptions), the 24-hour drafted agenda, the 60-minute session with sealed first takes, the five options, and the numbered Record of Decisions (SDS-2026-014).
4. Governance and trust. Every recommendation traces back to approved strategic memory and dated sources. EU-only infrastructure, GDPR by design.
5. Getting started ("You teach it what relevant means", four to five hours total) and closing call to action.

**Tone and look.** Cool, forensic, crisp. Editorial data restraint with clear typographic hierarchy, clean hairline diagrams and ample Paper whitespace.
